# TravelPlay01 independent presentation review

2026-10-04. Scope: new white surrounds on the existing cyan travel rings, their labels and placement in the authored world; the cube presentation is a secondary observation. This is independent visual critique, not implementation or physical interaction acceptance.

## Candidate Android verdict

**REVISE one small visual pass; retain the open-aperture concept and improved distant visibility.** I inspected all seven actual Unity Android renders in the local, ignored `Validation/CoastalWorld/TravelPlay01/Android` directory and read `BirdTravelPlayAuthoring.cs`. This review uses Dana's new Wailing Stone geometry reference, explicitly excluding its colors, together with the established bright matte-white architectural direction. The captures are editor/runtime evidence, not actual Quest views.

The new surrounds make the water target in `05` and upper targets in `06` much easier to distinguish than the former floating cyan ring alone. The aperture remains visually recognizable from these steep views. Front, back and oblique labels (`02`–`04`) are correctly oriented, readable at the sampled close distance and separated from the shell surface. “Pass Bird through, then click” is consistent with the latest requested point-crossing interaction; the image alone does not prove that behavior. The existing client/test panel overlaps the left area of the back view, so that view is not a wholly unobstructed presentation shot.

The main deficiency is the shell's treatment in `01`–`04`: its broad face is almost uniformly bright white and appreciably brighter than the surrounding architecture. Even the oblique view gives little sense of material or thickness. It reads as a large flat sign or appliance-like plate more than a matte sculpted monolith. At arrival it also competes with the Bird pickup centerpiece. The quiet white palette is appropriate; the lack of modeled light/value across the face and edge is the problem.

The reference's defining silhouette is tall and tapered, with the hole high in a substantial lower body. This candidate is comparatively squat: the source outline is about 3.1 m wide and 2.93 m high, surrounding the unchanged 1.79 m outside-diameter hoop. It captures a tapered shoulder and circular opening, but only partially captures the reference's proportions. A literal navel-height replica cannot contain the retained hoop at its existing size. Do not imply that literal scale was achieved.

## Bounded correction

1. **Prioritize subtle form shading.** Keep neutral white but make the broad face, edge bevel and aperture throat read as connected matte surfaces. Gentle economical vertex-value modeling is sufficient for a first correction; a new lighting system or decorative texture is unnecessary. Check that the shell stops looking self-lit beside the pickup and that the oblique view reads as a shallow solid object.
2. If practical without reducing any protected aperture or targeting corridor, modestly narrow the side/base contour to lessen the squat outline. Do not extend a large lower body into the floor or routes, move the hoop, or force literal reference proportions. This is secondary to correcting the flat white presentation.

The visible aperture and instruction must remain the dominant interaction cue. Preserve the actual cyan ring dimensions, tested oblique crossing paths, supported return stance and landing clearances. Source places the new visible shell collider on layer 17; appearance as a solid mass should not be confused with proof of player collision or a clear walking circuit. The wider footprint and routes still require the implementer's pending checks.

## Revised Android candidate: retain as an editable first version

I inspected all seven refreshed Android views after the body narrowed from 3.1 to 2.8 m, its outer thickness increased from 0.24 to 0.48 m, face values gained modest directional variation, and both labels moved outward. **RETAIN this revision as an editable first version for the functionality delivery.** The original candidate's request for a small correction is satisfied enough to stop presentation iteration here, subject to the separate functional and clearance checks.

The right edge in the oblique view (`04`) now reads as a solid bevel/thickness rather than a paper plate. The narrower silhouette is less squat, and the face has modest form cues. Full bright neutral white is appropriate to Dana's explicit material preference; matching the older gray indoor lighting is not an acceptance requirement. The cyan aperture remains obvious. Both label faces stay readable and correctly oriented; no new label burial is visible. Distant water and elevated targets (`05`, `06`) retain the earlier discoverability improvement, and the aperture remains recognizable in these sampled views. The cubes show no material presentation change.

The result is still a shallow, fairly squat adaptation of the reference, with less substantial lower body than the Wailing Stone image. Some fine radial faceting is visible across the broad white face, and the arrival beacon remains visually prominent next to the pickup. These are honest remaining aesthetic limitations, not reasons to enlarge or darken it during this delivery. A later art pass can refine the face continuity and silhouette if needed after hands-on use. There is no need for an architectural overhaul or further score-driven iteration now.
